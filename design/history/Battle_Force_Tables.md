# Isekai 825: Battle Force Tables

Oct 26, 2026 · @Neil Jensen

## How to read these

### The grid is the player's sector, not the battle

The 3×5 grid holds **4–8 units a side**, about 600–1,200 men at 20–100 per unit. Fontenoy had tens of thousands. So the grid is the stretch of field the player can see and influence; the rest is narrated around him.

This is not a concession. Nobody at Fontenoy saw the whole battle — it was fought in three separate parts that could not see each other, which is why the sources describe it as three battles at once. A commander knew his own frontage and whatever a rider had told him an hour ago.

**Hard limits:** 18 units a side absolute (3 per cell × 6 deployment cells, matching the gang-up cap). 12 is a soft cap for playability; 4–8 is the working range.

### The abstract layer

Everything outside the sector resolves on its own and feeds the grid as morale modifiers and events:

| Trigger | Effect in the sector |
| --- | --- |
| Allied wing collapses elsewhere | Morale hit to the player's units; `undisciplined` units hit harder |
| Reinforcements arrive (Warin's column at Fontenoy) | Morale boost; enemy opposite begins to waver |
| The wider battle turns decisively | Morale swing, and pressure to conform — a sector that holds while the army breaks is cut off |
| Historical Momentum (§2 of the combat doc) | The wider battle moves toward its historical outcome regardless of the sector's result |

### What the player brings, by rank

Roughly one armed man per 3–4 manses, per the capitulary standard. The starting villa of 10 manses yields about 3 men — not a unit, which is why a minor noble serves *inside* someone else's.

| Rank | Holdings | Brings | Command tier | On the grid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor noble | \~10 manses | Himself + 2–3 men | Rank and File | Attached to an allied Caballarii unit (§4.1); Elite Foot if serving in a great lord's household |
| Viscount | \~40–60 manses | 1 unit (usually Caballarii) | Sub-Command | His own unit, inside an allied sector |
| Count | A county | 3–4 units | Sub-Command, sometimes Full | A wing of the sector |
| Duke | Several counties | 6–8 units | Full Command | Most or all of the sector |
| King | A kingdom | 8–12 units | Full Command | The sector is his household and guard; the abstract layer is the rest of his own army |

**The convergence:** at the start the sector is a few units of someone else's army and the player is one man in it. By duke rank the sector *is* his contingent. By king rank sector and battle have nearly merged. That progression is the campaign's arc expressed in the combat system.

### Reading an entry

Each battle gives the historical scale (flavour, and what the abstract layer is doing), the **enemy sector force**, and the **allied sector force broken out by the player's rank**.

**The sector total stays roughly fixed; the player's own contingent displaces allied units one for one.** A villa lord fights inside someone else's army and the sector is almost entirely allied. A count brings 3–4 units and half the sector is his. A duke brings 6–8 and the allies are a rump. That is the whole progression: more is expected of you as you rise, and what you bring replaces what the king or your lord would otherwise have had to find.

**Three exceptions,** noted where they apply:

- **Named allies are never displaced.** Robert the Strong at Brissarthe, Bishop Géran at Saint-Florentin, Gaucelm at Chalon: they are there in their own right.
- **Siege engines stay royal assets** unless the player builds his own.
- **Home defence has no allies at any rank** — only what the player has raised.

### King rank

The throne is reachable from 888 (the open throne, Arc 8) or through the king quest at any point after. **A king's sector is read as the duke row**: he brings 8–12 units on paper, but the sector grid still holds 4–8, so what appears on it is his household and guard — the same 6 or so units a duke fields — while the rest of his army is the abstract layer.

Two differences from the duke row:

- **Allied units on the grid are his own men**, not a neighbour's: household Heavy Horse and Elite Foot rather than levied Caballarii.
- **He commands by right**, so Full Command is automatic rather than offered.

King rows are given explicitly on the battles that can be fought after 888.

### Unit persistence between battles

Casualties are permanent (combat doc §8.6: no HP recovery), but almost every engagement here is years apart, so a player musters fresh each time and the rank rows can be read as what he actually fields.

**Two exceptions where losses carry:**

- **The Saône campaign (summer 880) runs straight into the siege of Vienne that autumn.** Units come out of Mâcon and Lyon damaged and go to Vienne that way.
- **The Burgundy raiding seasons (886–887) are consecutive.** A militia mauled by foragers in one season is still weak the next.

### Unit note

**Ship Crew uses the Pedites/Fyrd stat line, Norse-skinned** (combat doc §7.10) — not Peasant Levy. A raider is a volunteer out for loot and status with proper spear, shield and gambison. The chieftain's own ship fields Elite Foot instead.

## 832–845

### 832 — Aquitaine and Gothia skirmishes (832-03)

Small actions, not pitched battles: a fortified estate, an ambush in the hills. **Scale:** a few hundred a side.

| **Enemy** | 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Caballarii, 1 Sagittarii |

### 841 — Fontenoy (841-02) — fixed point

The decade's great battle. **Scale:** tens of thousands; three separate parts of the field that cannot see each other. Momentum 55, but a **fixed point**: Charles and Louis win regardless.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Lothair and Pippin II)** | 2 Heavy Horse, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Caballarii, 1 Sagittarii |
| Allies — villa lord | 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii; player attached inside a Caballarii |
| Allies — viscount | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Allies — count | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Sagittarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — duke | 1 Heavy Horse + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii |

**At every rank the sector is still only one of three parts of the field.** Fontenoy is tens of thousands of men; the other two parts arrive as morale events from the abstract layer.

**If the player is in Warin's relief column** (the southern army whose late arrival turns the battle), his sector is separate and arrives in the battle's last third against an enemy already worn down:

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Lothair's flank, tiring)** | 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Caballarii — all at reduced morale after hours of fighting |
| Relief column — villa lord | 2 Caballarii (southern and Burgundian levies), 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Pedites/Fyrd; player attached |
| Relief column — viscount | 1 Caballarii, 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Relief column — count | 1 Heavy Horse (Warin) + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Relief column — duke | 1 Heavy Horse (Warin, never displaced) + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd |

**Weighted to the player here**, the reverse of the main line. This is the decisive arrival, so renown is correspondingly large — but the result is the same either way: Charles and Louis win, fixed point.

**Weighted to the enemy** for the first two-thirds — historically Lothair had the better of it until Warin arrived. **The abstract layer carries the battle:** news of the other two parts of the field arrives as morale events, and Warin's column appearing is the swing. If the player is *in* Warin's relief force, his sector arrives late against a tiring enemy and is weighted to him instead.

**Terrain:** rolling ground, a stream between the lines (+1 movement to cross).

### 845 — Ballon (845-B1)

Charles beaten in marshland near Redon. **Scale:** \~3,000 Franks against fewer, mostly light Breton horse.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Nominoë)** | 3 Breton/Gascon Light Cavalry, 2 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — villa lord | 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii; player attached |
| Allies — viscount | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Allies — count | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Sagittarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — duke | 1 Heavy Horse + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii |

**Rank does not help here.** More units of Frankish heavy horse in a swamp is more units bogged down. A duke who brings six is a duke with six stuck columns unless he adapts.

**Terrain is the enemy:** Swamp across most of the field — double movement, and the DEF bonus scales with the defender's own armour, so the Frankish heavy units bog down exactly as they historically did. Breton light cavalry with `hit_and_run` are nearly untouchable in it.

**Weighted to the enemy.** Momentum 70: the defeat stands. Renown comes from getting units out intact.

## 851–880

### 851 — Jengland (851-B1)

The arc's main engagement and a different kind of defeat: the Bretons refuse to close and ride the Franks to death over two days. **Scale:** Charles outnumbered them perhaps four to one and still lost.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Erispoe)** | 4 Breton/Gascon Light Cavalry, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — villa lord | 2 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Elite Foot (Saxon auxiliaries); player attached |
| Allies — viscount | 2 Heavy Horse, 1 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Elite Foot + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Allies — count | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Elite Foot, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — duke | 1 Elite Foot (Saxons) + player's 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd |

**The higher the rank, the worse the arithmetic.** Bringing more heavy cavalry to a battle where heavy cavalry cannot catch anything means more horses dying for nothing. A duke who fields light cavalry or dismounts is the one who comes out of this intact.

**The player's side is numerically stronger and loses anyway.** Every Breton unit has `hit_and_run`: they fire javelins and withdraw before the Franks can engage. The Frankish heavy units cannot catch them (Speed 5 against 6), and `skirmish_and_withdraw` deals damage without return damage. Two days of that is the historical attrition.

**Counters the player may find:** his own light cavalry to screen, dismounting to reduce the mobility gap, Sagittarii or Crossbowmen to make the withdrawal costly, terrain that denies the Bretons room. A commander who adapts comes out with a reputation while the king's army falls apart.

**Terrain:** open ground near the Vilaine, which favours the skirmishers. Momentum 65 — Charles still makes terms.

A small, ugly scramble, not a battle line: Vikings and Bretons shut in a stone church, the Franks besieging them, a sudden sortie.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Hastein's band and Bretons)** | 2 Ship Crew, 1 Elite Foot (Hastein's hirth), 1 Breton/Gascon Light Cavalry |
| Allies — villa lord | 2 Heavy Horse (Robert's and Ranulf's retinues), 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd; player attached |

**Robert's and Ranulf's Heavy Horse stay on the field at every rank** — this is Robert's campaign, and he is the man the player may be trying to save.

**Fortifications:** one Stone point (the church), `makeshift` — a building, not a fortress. The enemy Presents one unit; the rest are Stored.

**The shape of it:** the Franks are besieging a building and have the numbers. The danger is the sortie — a sudden breakout against a force that has stood down in the summer heat. **Robert can be saved** by a strong damage ratio keeping the enemy bottled, or by the armour situation card. Momentum 55.

### 870 — Siege of Vienne (870-C2)

Charles against Girart; months of investment, the city falls in December.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Defenders (Girart)** | 1 Elite Foot (Presented), 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii, 1 Crossbowmen |
| Besiegers — villa lord | 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Traction Trebuchet, 1 Penthouse Ram, 1 Siege Tower; player attached |
| Besiegers — viscount | 1 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd + the 3 engines + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Besiegers — count | 1 Pedites/Fyrd + the 3 engines + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Besiegers — duke | the 3 engines (royal assets) + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii |

**Siege engines are royal assets, not the player's**, unless he builds his own — which is precisely what a counterweight trebuchet would be.

**Fortifications:** Stone, properly maintained — Vienne is a Roman city with real walls. Three points across the face.

**Weighted to the defender**, which is why it took months. The player shortens it with better engineering, cutting the water supply, or a counterweight trebuchet if he has one (ATK 40 against Structural DEF 20 breaches in a handful of shots rather than dozens). Momentum 70: Vienne falls regardless; the player changes how fast and how bloody.

### 876 — Andernach (876-C1)

A night attack in the rain on a camp that has stood down. **Scale:** a royal army destroyed; Charles escapes with a handful of riders.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy (Louis the Younger)** | 3 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — villa lord | 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd, all Disordered; player attached |
| Allies — viscount | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Allies — count | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — duke | 1 Heavy Horse + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd |

**Rank changes how much the player can save, not whether he is ambushed.** Every allied unit starts Disordered with a surprise morale hit; the player's own units do too unless he took precautions on the march.

**Special conditions:** Weather is Rain. The allied units begin with a surprise morale hit and no Command Pool spent pre-battle (there was no deployment — the camp was asleep). The enemy enters with full `shock` charges available.

**Weighted overwhelmingly to the enemy.** The objective is survival: get units off the field intact. **The costly-victory route** (momentum 60) lets a strong performance hold the field, but Charles takes a wound that turns bad or falls sick, the eastern campaign collapses anyway, and he dies in 877 as he did.

## 880–911

The player is in his seventies and eighties here: **command tiers only** (§4.1 age rules in the scripts doc). He may run a siege by letter.

### 880–882 — Siege of Vienne, Boso's capital (880-K3)

Two years of investment; the city comes to terms rather than being stormed.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Defenders (Ermengard's garrison)** | 1 Elite Foot (Presented), 2 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii, 1 Crossbowmen, 1 Peasant Levy |
| Besiegers — villa lord | 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Traction Trebuchet, 1 Penthouse Ram, 1 Siege Tower; player attached |
| Besiegers — viscount | 1 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd + the 3 engines + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Besiegers — count | 1 Pedites/Fyrd + the 3 engines + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Besiegers — duke (holding Autun) | the 3 engines + player's 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd, 1 Sagittarii; two kings and an emperor are in camp |

**Fortifications:** Stone, three points across the face. The same Roman walls as 870 — if the player besieged Vienne ten years earlier, he knows them.

**Weighted to the defender**, heavily: two years is the historical answer. **This is the counterweight trebuchet's showcase.** ATK 40 unreduced against Structural DEF 20 gives `hit_multiplier` 0.67 and roughly 11 damage a shot against 150 Point HP — about 14 shots, or a season, against the two years a traction engine needs. Consequence: everyone who sees it copies it (see the Technology Diffusion doc).

**Multi-day rules (§8.6) matter here more than anywhere.** This is a campaign of day-long engagements across two years, not one fight.

| Besiegers — king | 1 Traction Trebuchet + the king's own 1 Elite Foot, 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd; taking Sens is now a royal campaign, not a magnate's land grab |

**Fortifications:** Stone, `makeshift` — Sens has old walls in indifferent repair. Weighted to the attacker; this is a short campaign, not a two-year siege.

### 898 — Argenteuil-sur-Armançon (898-V1)

A winter surprise attack on a camp. The best tactical situation the player gets all game.

| Side | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemy** | 3 Ship Crew, 1 Elite Foot (all Disordered if surprise lands) |
| Allies — villa lord | 1 Heavy Horse (Richard), 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd; player attached |
| Allies — viscount | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 1 Caballarii |
| Allies — count | 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Pedites/Fyrd + player's 2 Caballarii, 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Allies — duke (holding Autun) | none, or 1 Pedites/Fyrd from the bishop + player's 1 Heavy Horse, 2 Caballarii, 3 Pedites/Fyrd; **he plans and commands both actions** |
| Allies — king | 1 Pedites/Fyrd (the bishop's) + the king's own 1 Heavy Horse, 1 Elite Foot, 2 Caballarii, 2 Pedites/Fyrd |

**Conditions:** winter (ships far off, no escape by water), surprise if the approach is handled well, enemy camped rather than formed. Supply costs double for the attacker (winter campaigning) and morale takes a cold penalty.

**Weighted to the player if surprise lands**, roughly even if it does not. Momentum 30. **The largest renown of the late game**, and if the player holds Autun on the Duke track, he plans and commands both this and Saint-Florentin.

### 911 — Chartres (911-F1)

The player is about 102. He **sends men**; there is no sector for him at any rank, king included. Resolve abstractly and report through Aldric. A king's contribution is a named contingent in the coalition (1 Heavy Horse, 1 Elite Foot, 2 Caballarii under a captain) and the credit or blame for sending it.

## Local defence and the king quest

### Local encounters (no summons)

These scale to what the player has actually raised, which is the point: they are the test of his own preparation, not of an army's.

| Encounter | When | Enemy force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deserters plundering a village** (the tutorial battle) | 830 | 1 Peasant Levy or understrength Pedites/Fyrd, 10–20 men, no cohesion |
| **A neighbour's land grab** | 830, 880s | 1 Pedites/Fyrd + 1 Caballarii (his retinue) |
| **Lothair's foragers** | 834 | 1 Pedites/Fyrd understrength; then 1 Pedites/Fyrd + 1 Caballarii |
| **Deserters after Fontenoy** | 841 | 1–2 Pedites/Fyrd, Disordered, no command |
| **Viking foraging party** | 886–898 | 1 Ship Crew, understrength |
| **Viking raid in force** | 886–887 | 2 Ship Crew |
| **Bandits** | any, worse in famine years | 1 Peasant Levy, `improvised_weapons` |

| Player's rank | What he fields |
| --- | --- |
| Villa lord, no militia | 1–2 Peasant Levy (his own people, badly armed) |
| Villa lord, militia trained | 1–2 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Villa lord, militia and horses | + 1 Caballarii |

**No allies at any rank.** This is the test of the player's own preparation, not of an army's.

**The tutorial battle is deliberately winnable** by a prepared player and survivable by an unprepared one — Peasant Levy against Peasant Levy, with the player's unit holding the better ground if he reacted to the warning.

### The king quest battle

One pitched battle per attempt, and the only one in the game where **the player's own inventions are in the field on both sides** (see the Technology Diffusion doc: anything given to an ally or used publicly has spread).

**Sector composition is decided by the support phase.** Each named ally won brings roughly 1–2 units to the player's sector; each one lost adds to the king's.

| Supporters won (of 9) | The player's sector | The king's sector |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7–8 | 4–6 allied units (see the composition table below) + the player's own 2–3 | 4–5 |
| 6 (parity) | 3–4 allied + the player's own 2–3 | 6 |
| 4–5 | 2–3 allied + the player's own 2 | 7–8 |
| 3 or fewer | 1–2 allied + the player's own 2 | 8+; he should not take the field |

**This is the one battle where allied strength rises with the player's own standing rather than falling** — the supporters he won are a direct product of his rank, reputation and sixty years of favours. A duke-rank player brings more units *and* attracts more allies.

**The betrayer turns mid-battle**, taking his contingent with him: 1–2 units leave the player's sector and reappear in the king's. At parity that is a swing from 6–6 to 5–7 at the worst possible moment, which is why a careful player wants seven supporters.

**Composition by source:**

| Ally type | What he brings |
| --- | --- |
| Great magnate (grievance, fear of the alternative) | 1 Heavy Horse + 1 Pedites/Fyrd |
| Bought lord (money) | 1 Caballarii, and may be outbid before the battle |
| Bishop (divine favour, debt) | 1 Pedites/Fyrd + 1 Sagittarii, and the legitimacy that matters more than the men |
| Frontier lord (protection) | 1 Caballarii + 1 Breton/Gascon Light Cavalry |
| The player's own household | 1–2 Elite Foot or Heavy Horse, plus whatever he has invented |

**The king's side** is drawn from royal household troops: Heavy Horse and Elite Foot as the core, Caballarii and Pedites/Fyrd from the counties still loyal, and — if diffusion has run its course — counterweight trebuchets, improved crossbows, or whatever else the player let escape his lands over the preceding decades.

**Do not kill the king** (see the scripts doc). Capture, forced abdication or flight are the clean outcomes; killing an anointed king is carried for the rest of the game.
