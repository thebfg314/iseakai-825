# Isekai 825: Rank Progression — Holdings and Powers

Oct 29, 2026 · @Neil Jensen

## Principles

### Everything built carries forward

A mill, a granary, drained fields, walls, vines, a trained militia: these are the player's own improvements to land he holds, and promotion never takes them. Fontenay remains his whatever else he gains. **Promotion adds; it does not replace.**

The one caveat is tenure. Land held as a benefice from a lord can be reassigned when that lord falls (see the 830 land crisis). Improvements to a benefice are improvements to someone else's property — which is the whole argument for buying the villa outright.

### Three things grow, and they grow differently

|  | How it scales |
| --- | --- |
| **Land and people** | Roughly ×3 per rank. More villas, not a bigger Fontenay — holdings were scattered, not consolidated |
| **Public revenue** | Appears at Count and then dominates. Tolls, market dues, a share of justice fines, mint profits. This is income from *office*, not from land |
| **Powers** | Step changes, not multiples. Each rank unlocks things the previous rank simply could not do |

### The shift that matters

At minor noble and viscount, income is **what the land produces**. At count and above, income is increasingly **what the office collects**. A count who loses his county loses most of his income even though he keeps every acre he owns, which is exactly why the king's escalation ladder works (see the scripts doc).

This also changes what the player manages. Early game is farming and improvement; late game is administration, patronage and war, with the villa running itself through Aldric.

### A note on the manse as a unit

The starting villa is 10 manses and about 177 acres, of which 32 are ploughland. A manse is a tenant farm with its family, not a fixed acreage. At higher ranks the player stops counting acres and counts manses and villas, because nobody could survey a county field by field — the estate inventories themselves list manses and what they owe.

## Viscount (100 renown)

Deputy to the count over part of a pagus. Still fundamentally a landholder, but now with men under him and a court to hold.

### Holdings

|  | Minor noble | Viscount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manses | 10 | **30–35** |
| Villas | 1 (Fontenay) | **2–3** — Fontenay plus one or two more, not adjacent |
| Ploughland | 32 acres | \~100 acres |
| Meadow | 8 | \~25 |
| Vineyard | 1 | \~4 |
| Orchard | 1 | \~3 |
| Woodland | 40 | \~120 |
| Scrub and waste | 80 | \~200 (room to clear) |
| Working people | 12 men, 13 women, 9 youths | \~35 men, \~38 women, \~27 youths |
| Labour days | 180 / 130 / 27 | \~540 / 390 / 80 |
| Yearly income | \~525 d | **\~1,600 d** |
| Chest (typical) | 138 d | 400–800 d |

**Stores scale with the land** — roughly ×3 on grain, livestock and raw materials. Cattle 5 → 15, sheep 14 → 40, horses 2 → 6.

### New on the books

| New stat | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Vassals** | 2–4 minor nobles holding benefices from him. They owe him service, and he answers for them. Each brings 2–3 armed men to a muster |
| **Court** | He hears small cases: boundaries, theft, disputed inheritance. Income from fines (small, 20–60 d a year), and a loyalty effect: judging fairly builds it, judging greedily spends it |
| **Deputy's share** | A cut of what he collects for the count. Modest, and it makes him the man tenants complain about |
| **Residence** | A fortified hall, or the beginnings of one. A Wooden fortification point at his seat (combat doc §9.4) |

### Military obligation

One armed man per 3–4 manses: **8–10 men**, which is one unit. He fields 1 Caballarii and serves at Sub-Command. His own vassals' men fold into it.

### What he can now do

- Hold a court, and make a reputation as a just or a grasping lord
- Take vassals, which is the first step toward a following of his own
- Fortify his seat
- Be summoned by name rather than as one of the county's men

## Count (500 renown)

The largest single jump in the game. A count is a **royal officer**, not merely a large landholder, and most of what he gains comes from the office rather than the acres.

### Holdings

|  | Viscount | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manses (his own) | 30–35 | **120–180** |
| Villas | 2–3 | **8–15**, scattered across the county and beyond |
| Comital fisc | — | The lands attached to the office, held while he holds it: **another 100–200 manses** |
| Working people | \~100 | **\~600–900** across all holdings |
| Income from land | \~1,600 d | **\~7,000–10,000 d** |
| Income from office | — | **\~5,000–12,000 d** (see below) |
| Chest (typical) | 400–800 d | 3,000–8,000 d |

**The fisc is the trap.** Those 100–200 manses are the office's, not his. Lose the county and they go with it, along with every public revenue below. Only his own 120–180 manses are safe.

### Public revenues — new income type

| Source | Typical yearly | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tolls** | 1,500–4,000 d | Bridges, fords, roads, river traffic. Scales with trade; collapses in a Viking year |
| **Market dues** | 800–2,000 d | A cut of sales at the county's markets |
| **Justice fines** | 1,000–3,000 d | The count keeps a third; the rest goes to the king. Rises with a strict court and falls with an unpopular one |
| **Mint share** | 0 or 500–2,000 d | Only with the right (see the scripts doc); Chalon has a mint from 864 |
| **Hospitality owed** | In kind | The county feeds him and his household as he travels. Real money saved, and resented if abused |

### New on the books

| New stat | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Officers** | A viscount of his own, an advocate (church cases), a vicar, a chamberlain. Each handles a function and each wants paying |
| **The county levy** | Not his men: the county's free men, called in the king's name. **200–400 men, 3–4 units.** He can call them for royal service freely; calling them for his own purposes costs loyalty |
| **Castle or comital hall** | Stone if he inherits Roman work, Wooden if built. 1–2 fortification points (combat doc §9.4) |
| **Lay abbacy** | Optional: hold an abbey's lands and revenues without taking orders. Large income, and the church's opinion of him turns on how he treats it |
| **Vassals** | 8–15 minor nobles and viscounts |
| **Seat at assemblies** | He attends the king's assemblies by right, which is how the political events in the scripts doc reach him directly rather than through Aldric |

### Military obligation

**His own men: 30–45** (3–4 units) from his manses. **Plus the county levy** of 200–400 when called in the king's name. He serves at Sub-Command or Full Command.

### What he can now do

- Collect public revenue, which untethers his income from the harvest
- Hold the high court: blood cases, not just boundaries
- Call the county to arms
- Appoint officers, and be appealed to by lesser lords
- Petition the king directly — and be summoned to answer directly

## Duke / Marquis (1000 renown)

Several counties under one hand. Historically this title was not granted so much as accumulated — Richard was called *dux* only after twenty years of holding counties, and the king confirmed what was already true.

### Holdings

|  | Count | Duke |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counties | 1 | **3–6** |
| Manses (his own) | 120–180 | **400–700** |
| Villas | 8–15 | **30–60** |
| Working people | \~600–900 | **\~3,000–5,000** |
| Income from land | \~7,000–10,000 d | **\~30,000–50,000 d** |
| Income from office | \~5,000–12,000 d | **\~25,000–50,000 d** across the counties |
| Chest (typical) | 3,000–8,000 d | 20,000–50,000 d |

### New on the books

| New stat | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Counts under him** | 2–5, each with their own fisc and levy. They are royal officers too, so this is influence, not ownership — the slow squeeze of 898-K1 is exactly this relationship being established |
| **Regional levy** | 1,000–2,000 men, 8–12 units, when the whole march turns out |
| **Marcher authority** | He can act without waiting for the king: raise troops, treat with neighbours, judge between counts. The practical meaning of Courtenot in 901 |
| **Mint** | Granted or taken (scripts doc). A steady profit and a statement about who rules here |
| **Multiple fortifications** | A seat in each major county; Stone where Roman work survives |
| **Patronage** | He appoints or sponsors men to counties, abbacies and bishoprics. The currency he pays supporters in during the king quest |

### Military obligation

**His own men: 100–170** (6–8 units on the grid). **The regional levy: 1,000–2,000.** Full Command by default.

## King (2000 renown)

Reaching 2000 makes the ambition plausible; taking the throne needs the king quest (scripts doc) or the open throne of 888.

### Holdings

|  | Duke | King |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The royal fisc | — | Palaces, forests, hundreds of villas across the kingdom — held by the crown, not the man |
| His own patrimony | 400–700 manses | Unchanged; this is what he keeps if deposed |
| Income | \~55,000–100,000 d | **Several hundred thousand d**, mostly from the fisc, tolls, mints and tribute |

**A deposed king keeps only his patrimony.** Charles the Fat went from the whole empire to nothing in six weeks.

### New on the books

| New stat | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **The royal fisc** | Palaces and estates across the kingdom, with the right to move between them and be fed by each |
| **Missi dominici** | Inspectors sent out in pairs to hear complaints and check his officers. His only real eyes outside the court |
| **Right to grant** | Counties, abbacies, immunities, minting rights, charters. The power that makes supporters |
| **The host** | He summons the whole kingdom, not a region: 5,000–20,000 men |
| **Assemblies** | He calls them, sets the agenda, and issues capitularies — the player can now make the kind of law the game has been reporting to him for sixty years |
| **The church** | He nominates bishops and abbots, with everything that implies for legitimacy |
| **Coinage** | The royal coinage, in his name |

### What changes in play

The villa still runs through Aldric; the sector on the battle grid is still 4–8 units (his household and guard). What changes is that the **events in the scripts doc now arrive as decisions rather than news**: the tribute to pay or refuse, the count to appoint, the campaign to call. The player becomes the man Aldric used to report on.

# Trade health

A single hidden number per region, 0–100, representing how much is actually moving on the roads and rivers. **The player never sees the number.** He sees merchants arriving or not, prices moving, and what his surplus fetches.

**Why hidden:** nobody in 825 had a trade index. Aldric saying "the Saône road is quiet this season, my lord, and the salt man hasn't come since Easter" is better than a bar, and it makes the player's own improvements feel discovered rather than read off a meter.

**The diegetic readout is Gerulf**, the negotiator already on the household roster. A trader knows the roads; asking him is a natural action and gives a qualitative answer ("thin, my lord, thinner than last year") rather than a figure.

### What the number does

| Effect | How it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Merchant visits** | Frequency of travelling merchants reaching the villa. At 70+, several a season; at 30, one or two a year; below 20, none |
| **Prices for the player's surplus** | What grain, wine, wool and hides fetch. The PRICES block on the books screen shifts within its stated ranges |
| **Availability** | Whether salt, iron, fine cloth, wine and luxuries can be bought at all |
| **Tolls and market dues** (Count+) | Direct multiplier on those revenue lines |
| **Rumour and news speed** | Merchants carry word; low trade means Aldric's reports arrive thinner and later |

### Starting value and baseline

**Autun region, 825: 55.** Inland Burgundy on the Saône–Rhône corridor, recovering from the 820–824 famine. Not a hub, not a backwater.

### Improvements the player can make

Suggested effects; tune against play. These are cumulative and mostly permanent.

| Improvement | Effect on trade health |
| --- | --- |
| Repair and maintain the local road | **+3**, and +1 a year while maintained |
| Build or repair a bridge or improve a ford | **+5** |
| Clear bandits; keep a standing watch | **+5**, and it prevents the −10 a banditry event would cause |
| Found a market at the villa (needs a licence from the count or king) | **+10**, and merchants arrive on a schedule rather than by chance |
| Found an annual fair | **+8** on top of a market, concentrated in one season |
| Build a mill, forge or other draw | **+3** each — people come for the service and trade while they are there |
| Hospitality for travellers (a guesthouse, stabling) | **+2**, and a reputation effect with the church |
| Produce something worth coming for (good wine, surplus wool, iron goods) | **+2 to +6** by scale |
| Standardised weights and measures | **+4** — merchants prefer a market where they are not cheated |
| Keep tolls low once held (Count+) | **+5** versus **−10** for tolls set greedily |

# Administration — abstracted through Aldric

**No cast of new officers.** Aldric is already the interface for everything else; he grows with the player rather than being surrounded by strangers. A count does not meet eight named deputies — he hears Aldric say *"I have put a man in at Beaune, my lord, a cousin of the miller's. He'll do."*

### How it works

One number: **administrative capacity**, against the number of holdings the player has. Shortfalls surface as Aldric's complaints, never as a management screen.

| Rank | Aldric is | Capacity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor noble | Steward of one villa | 1–2 holdings |
| Viscount | Steward with two or three under-stewards | 3–4 holdings |
| Count | Chief officer, with unnamed staff in each villa | 10–16 holdings |
| Duke | Head of a household administration | 40–60 holdings, through deputies he appoints himself |
| King | Chancellor in all but name | The fisc, through the *missi* |

### When capacity is exceeded

Effects are gradual, and Aldric says so before the numbers bite:

| Overload | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 holdings over | Income from the furthest holdings drops 10%; Aldric sounds tired |
| 3–5 over | 20% drop; a holding's dues go uncollected for a season; small leakage |
| 6+ over | 30% drop, visible corruption, a holding effectively running itself |

**The fixes:** promote Aldric (raises capacity a tier), let him hire staff (money, +2–3 holdings), appoint a deputy for a region (money and a loyalty risk), or simply hold less.

### The few named officers

Two or three across the whole game, introduced only where the story needs them:

| Officer | When | Why named |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Captain of the guard** | When the player first raises a militia (the books screen already says "No captain of the guard yet") | He leads units the player does not personally command — and leads them when the player is too old to ride |
| **A regional deputy** | Duke rank | He is competent and ambitious; whether that becomes useful or dangerous is a thread |
| **Aldric's successor** | Optional, c. 880 | His son, same name and voice; one line of acknowledgement, no new personality |

# Vassals

Shown as stylised figures with a mood, in a row beneath the peasant happiness meter.

| Rank | Vassals | Display |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor noble | 0 | — |
| Viscount | 2–4 | Individual figures |
| Count | 8–15 | Individual figures up to 12 |
| Duke | Counts and their vassals | **Groups**, not individuals: "the Auxois lords," "the Nivernais lords" as blocks with a shared mood, plus 2–3 named ones who matter |
| King | The magnates | Groups, plus the named great men |

**Why groups at the top:** a duke did not think about fifteen individual vassals; he thought about regions and about the handful of men who could cause him trouble. The display should match.

### What moves vassal mood

Distinct from tenant loyalty: tenants want feeding and protecting, vassals want rewarding and fearing.

| Up | Down |
| --- | --- |
| Land, offices, a good marriage | Service demanded with no reward |
| Being led to victory and loot | Being led to defeat, or left out of a victory |
| Judging in their favour | Judging for their tenants against them |
| Protection when threatened | Failing to answer when they are attacked |
| Confirming their sons in their holdings | Taking a holding back |

**Where it pays out:** the nine supporters of the king quest are this system cashing in. A player with contented vassals across forty years has a following; one who used them has nine conversations that go badly.

# Peers — deliberately not a system

Neighbouring counts and other magnates have **no tracked relationship**. Their attitudes are held narratively, by the LLM, from what has actually happened.

**The one exception is Richard**, once he is the player's overlord or rival — the slow squeeze of 898-K1 needs a real relationship. That is one named NPC with a tracked attitude, not a system.

Everything else that peer relations would have produced lives in the king quest's nine supporters, which is where those decisions are actually interesting.

# Justice

Justice is not an income line. It is a lever that pays in money and reputation in **opposite directions**, and the player chooses which he wants.

### Two modes, by rank

| Rank | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Villa lord, viscount** | **The player judges cases himself.** 1–2 a season, drawn from the dispute table. At 10 manses and \~34 working adults, a handful of disputes a year is what actually happened — the volume is naturally small, and judging them is immersive and teaches the stance system through real decisions |
| **Count and above** | **The player sets a stance** and his officers handle the routine. He still personally judges **1–2 cases a year**: the ones too large or too political for a deputy. Each such case opens with a line establishing that many others were heard and this is the one that mattered |

### The stance (introduced at Count)

Set once and changeable, with a tooltip on first appearance explaining why it exists: *"You can no longer hear every case yourself — a county has thousands of people. Your officers judge in your name. How they judge is up to you."*

| Stance | Fines collected | Tenant and county loyalty | Church | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lenient** | −50% | **+2 a year** | Approves | Disorder rises; crime goes unpunished; neighbours test your borders |
| **Fair** | Baseline | **+1 a year** | Approves | None. The slow, safe choice |
| **Strict** | +30% | **−1 a year** | Neutral | Resentment accumulates quietly |
| **Extortionate** | **+100%** | **−3 a year** | Condemns | *Missi* investigate; a profitable court is exactly what the capitularies complain of. Feeds the king's escalation ladder — a count whose people dislike him is the one who can be stripped |

**The historical point worth preserving:** a count kept a third of the fines and sent the rest to the king. Profiting from justice was the standing complaint of the period, and the *missi dominici* existed largely to check it.

### Dispute tables

Three tables, one per tier. The cases differ in kind, not just scale.

#### Villa lord and viscount — 1–2 a season

| Dispute |
| --- |
| A boundary stone has been moved; both families swear it was always where they say |
| A pig got into a neighbour's garden and ruined the beans |
| Two men claim the same strip in the open field after a death |
| A betrothal agreed and now refused; the bride-price has been spent |
| A theft of tools from the forge; the accused is a good worker with a bad reputation |
| A tenant says he cannot pay his dues this year; his neighbours say he can |
| A dog has killed sheep; whose dog, and who pays |
| A man struck another at the harvest feast; there is blood but no death |
| Timber taken from the woodland without leave — the player's own woodland |
| A widow's holding: her husband's brother wants it, she wants to keep it |
| Two families disagree over the use of a well or stream |
| A marriage within the forbidden degrees; Ermenald is unhappy; the couple are not |

#### Count — 1–2 a year, from many heard

Each entry opens with a framing line: *"You hear dozens of cases at the county court this season. This is the one that will be remembered."*

| Dispute |
| --- |
| A killing between two noble families; both demand justice, neither will accept a wergild |
| An abbey and a lay lord both claim a villa; the abbey has a charter, the lord has possession |
| An inheritance split among three noble brothers, one of them your own vassal |
| A vassal accused by his own tenants of taking more than his due |
| A merchant robbed on the road and demanding you compensate him, since the road is yours to keep safe |
| A man claims he is free; his lord claims he is not. The proof is a lost charter |
| A bishop's advocate and a count's officer, each claiming jurisdiction over the same village |
| A disputed mill: who may build one on the river, and who must grind where |
| An accusation of sorcery against a woman the village fears; Ermenald urges caution, the village does not |
| A royal vassal refuses your court entirely, claiming he answers only to the king |

#### Duke and king — 1 a year at most

Same framing, higher stakes: these are cases that could not be settled below.

| Dispute |
| --- |
| Two counts in open quarrel over a border district; both have armed men out |
| An appeal against one of your own officers, accused of extortion. Judging against him costs you a servant; judging for him costs you the county's trust |
| A bishop against a count, over a church's lands. Rome may hear of it |
| A monastery asking confirmation of immunity — freedom from your own officers' jurisdiction |
| A succession disputed between a dead count's son and his brother, with both parties' men gathering |
| A case the king has sent to you to decide, which is a test of whether you will rule in his interest or your own |

### What judging feeds

| Outcome | Affects |
| --- | --- |
| Fairness over time | Tenant loyalty, county loyalty, the "can he be stripped" check in the king's escalation ladder |
| Favouring the church | `favor_church`, Ermenald's trust |
| Favouring the powerful | Vassal loyalty up, tenant loyalty down |
| Taking bribes or heavy fines | Money now, `missi` attention later |
| Refusing to judge at all | Disputes fester into feuds; a feud is a future event, not a closed case |

## The Autun variant

The only holding that differs materially. A Duke-rank player may hold it from 879–880 (see the scripts doc). It is richer than a generic county for four reasons:

| Feature | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Roman walls** | The seat is a **Stone** fortification point, not Wooden — the single biggest defensive upgrade available (Point HP 150, Structural DEF 20, Defender bonus +20) |
| **A cathedral city and bishop** | Church influence concentrated in the player's own seat. Doubles the weight of `favor_church` locally, and the bishop is a political actor at his gate |
| **Chalon's mint, 50 km off (from 864)** | Trade health +8 regionally, and the easiest mint right in the kingdom to acquire |
| **The Saône corridor** | Tolls run **+40%** over a generic county: the road south to Lyon, Provence and Italy passes through |

**Net:** about **+25% total income** over a generic county of the same size, a Stone seat, and a bishop to manage. It is also the most contested county in the region — the counts list in the scripts doc shows it changing hands nine times in eighty years.

## What carries over

**Everything the player built stays his.** When the books screen gains a new rank's holdings, these come with him unchanged:

| Improvement | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| Mills, granaries, forge, oratory | Yes — and now he can build them on new villas too |
| Drained fields, cleared scrub, ridged ploughland | Yes; the acreage is simply part of the larger total |
| Vines and orchards | Yes — Burgundian wine is a trade good worth scaling |
| Walls and watch posts | Yes, and they become one fortification point among several |
| Trained militia | Yes — it becomes the core of his first proper units |
| Introduced techniques (rotation, drainage, ergot sorting) | Yes, and they now spread across every villa he holds — see the Technology Diffusion doc |
| Household NPCs (Aldric, Ermenald, Bernard, Gerulf) | Yes; they gain staff beneath them rather than being replaced |
| Relics, church standing, reputation, flags | Yes, all of it |
| **Benefice land held from a lord** | **Only while that lord stands.** The 830 crisis is the lesson |

## What the books screen should add

| At rank | New sections or lines |
| --- | --- |
| Viscount | **Vassals** (names, holdings, men owed); **Court** (cases pending, fines collected); second villa listed under LAND |
| Count | **The county** (its villas, manses, people); **Public revenue** (tolls, market, justice, mint); **Officers** (names and functions); **The levy** (men available, when last called); **Fisc vs. patrimony** clearly separated |
| Duke | **Counties held**; **Counts under him** (names, loyalty); **Patronage** (appointments made and owed); **Mint** if held |
| King | **The royal fisc** (palaces, where he is in residence); **Missi** (sent, reporting); **Grants** (made, pending); **The host** |

**One line that should appear from Count onward:** a clear split between what he owns and what he merely holds by office. That distinction is the spine of the late game, and the player should never be able to forget it.
